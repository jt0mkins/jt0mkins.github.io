RF Rocket Downlink University of Canterbury Aerospace Club

Overview

For my final year honours project, I designed and built a long-range LoRa telemetry system for the UC Aerospace hybrid rocket, paired with a dynamic link budget analysis that predicts signal margin across the full flight profile. As sole author I owned it end to end: radio PCB, RF matching, antenna, firmware and the ground station data pipeline, through to manufactured hardware on the bench.

The Project

The brief was to stream flight data from the rocket to the ground station, and to understand how the link behaves. Key constraints:

  • Dynamic link: range, altitude and orientation change path loss and antenna gain in flight, so a static budget is not enough
  • Regulatory: within the NZ ISM band and its power limits
  • Integration: CAN interface to the flight computer, one board for flight and ground
  • Field use: live data to the team at a remote site with no internet

How I Built It

I designed a custom radio PCB in KiCad around an STM32H5 microcontroller and a Semtech SX1262 LoRa transceiver, operating at 915 MHz. The board receives flight data from the flight computer over CAN and drives the radio over SPI. I calculated the RF matching network for the SX1262 at 915 MHz.

The same PCB is used on both the rocket and the ground station. That meant only one board to design, assemble and bring up, and one firmware codebase to maintain. Any fault found on one end was fixed on both, which cut debugging time. It also gave me a known-good reference board for bench testing, and boards can be swapped between ends as spares.

The J-pole antenna was designed as its own PCB with a 256 mm radiator, deliberately long to leave trim allowance for VNA tuning onto frequency. I simulated the Gerber geometry in NEC2 to validate it. On the ground a Yagi feeds the receive board, which streams telemetry into a custom-built dashboard so the team can monitor the flight live.

Alongside the hardware I built a dynamic link budget model that tracks received power and link margin through the flight rather than at a single worst-case point.

Results

The telemetry PCBs were manufactured and brought up on the bench, and the J-pole antenna was brought up on the VNA for tuning. The project delivered a reusable flight and ground radio platform for UC Aerospace and a method for predicting telemetry performance before launch.

Landing Bay Camera System Dawn Aerospace

Overview

During my internship at Dawn Aerospace I independently designed and delivered a complete landing gear camera system for the Mk-II B reusable suborbital spaceplane. The project spanned mechanical design, PCB development and firmware. I owned a real flight hardware system end to end, from first principles through to bench-tested integration.

The Project

The brief was to fit an FPV camera capable of recording 4K 60fps footage into the landing gear bay of the spaceplane, controlled directly by the flight computer. The constraints were significant:

  • Size: the PCB and enclosure had to fit within the tight geometry of the landing gear bay
  • Waterproofing: connector selection and enclosure design had to protect against moisture ingress
  • Thermal: the camera tended to overheat, so it needed careful thermal management in an enclosed space
  • Serviceability: the enclosure had to be easy to install and remove every flight

How I Built It

I designed a custom STM32-based PCB to bridge the flight computer's GPIO output to the camera's UART interface. It handles power, recording control and camera configuration, including framerate and resolution. The firmware includes a configuration mode that sets camera parameters on initialisation.

I designed the mechanical enclosure in CAD by enveloping the components and modelling directly to the internal surface geometry of the landing gear bay, which gave a precise fit. It was manufactured in nylon, chosen for its strength-to-weight properties and machinability, with waterproof connectors selected to meet the environmental requirements.

Results

Full bench testing confirmed the system met every design requirement end to end. The GPIO to UART conversion operated cleanly, thermal management kept the camera within operating temperature, the waterproofing held up under testing, and the enclosure installed and uninstalled cleanly within the intended workflow.

Young Wāhine in STEM Solar-Powered RC Car Program

Overview

Young Wāhine in STEM is an outreach program I created from scratch in partnership with EVolocity and Orion Energy. It introduces high school girls to engineering through a hands-on, end-to-end build project. The pilot ran across 5 Christchurch schools with 26 students, and it is planned for national rollout the following year.

5schools
26students
3disciplines
1race day

The Project

I designed the curriculum around a solar-powered RC car. I chose it because it covers all three core engineering disciplines in a way that's tangible and exciting for students with no prior background:

Mechanical

Students followed CAD documentation to design components of their car

Electrical

Students soldered a custom PCB designed for the program

Software

Students wrote the embedded code to control their RC car

The program ended with a race day. Every student had a real finish line to work toward and got to see their build perform.

My Role

I led the program end to end: scoping the project, writing the curriculum and documentation, coordinating with partner organisations and facilitating the workshops myself. My mechatronics background shaped how the project was structured. I could draw on real engineering practice to decide what level of challenge was appropriate and how to scaffold each discipline accessibly.

What I Learnt

Running live workshops taught me a lot about the gap between a plan and reality. I had to observe quickly, make calls on what was and wasn't working, and refine content and delivery between sessions, sometimes between workshops in the same week. It was a strong exercise in rapid iteration under constraints I couldn't fully control: school schedules, varying student experience levels and time pressure. Communicating technical ideas clearly to a non-engineering audience turned out to be one of the most valuable skills I built. Knowing something and being able to teach it are very different, and this program pushed me hard on that.

1kW Electric Go Kart EVolocity

What

  • Completed as a Year 13 student with no prior electronics experience
  • Built a 1kW electric go kart for the EVolocity national competition
  • Led Arduino embedded systems and 3D printed component design

How

  • Designed lightweight mounts in Fusion 360 and 3D printed them to fit the frame
  • Built an Arduino monitoring system using current and voltage sensing for real-time battery state of charge
  • Implemented IR-based speed sensing with interrupt handling and debouncing
  • Streamed live data via Bluetooth serial to two onboard LCD displays

Results

  • 1st place in Drag Race, Efficiency and Street Circuit at the Auckland national event
  • Arduino system delivered real-time battery and performance data throughout racing
  • Custom 3D prints held structural loads while staying lightweight

Weightlifting Form Analysis Computer Vision

I developed a computer vision tool for kinematic analysis of weightlifting movements. It processes recorded video to extract 3D joint coordinates and track 33 body landmarks, then calculates dynamic joint angles and bar path verticality.

Using error-detection heuristics, the tool identifies biomechanical deviations such as lumbar rounding or asymmetric loading. It's built with Python and OpenCV and turns raw footage into actionable metrics for improving technique.
